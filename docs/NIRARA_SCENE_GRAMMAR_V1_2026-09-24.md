# NIRARA — SCENE GRAMMAR v1

**Date:** 2026-09-24  
**Status:** CANONICAL ASSEMBLY LAW  
**Purpose:** Give NIRARA a repeatable visual language without turning it into a fixed template.

---

# 1. PRINCIPLE

NIRARA is assembled, not generated whole.

The scene is built from real fragments, but the fragments are not assigned fixed meanings.

A head can become a witness, a shadow, a stain or part of the centre.

Concrete can become wall, skin, floor, cavity or body.

Cloth can become debris, flesh, darkness or architecture.

The artist does not “place assets.”

The artist:
- moves;
- crops;
- hides;
- repeats;
- erases;
- scales;
- stains;
- distorts;
- overlaps;
- rotates;
- breaks;
- recolours;
- buries;
- reveals;
- combines;
- destroys the original identity of fragments.

The goal is for the source pieces to stop reading as pieces and become one field.

---

# 2. CORE SCENE CONDITIONS

A valid NIRARA composition must contain four forces.

## A — WITNESS FIELD

A surrounding or upper-biased field of watching presences.

Not necessarily a crowd.

The witness field may contain:
- full heads;
- partial heads;
- one visible eye;
- a face almost swallowed by darkness;
- repeated fragments;
- fragments that merge into architecture.

Rules:
- witnesses are not distributed evenly;
- not every witness is equally legible;
- some may be almost lost;
- some may be too close;
- some may be too far;
- their gaze should create pressure, not decoration.

---

## B — THE CRY

A small distressed presence, usually biased toward the lower-left / marginal field.

The crying presence should:
- remain smaller than the dominant witness field;
- feel physically isolated;
- disturb the local material / colour / light;
- produce intensity without becoming a mascot;
- remain unanswered by the rest of the scene.

The cry may be visible, sonic or both.

Its visual force can come from:
- posture;
- mouth;
- body compression;
- trembling;
- local colour break;
- wetness;
- flare;
- blur;
- scraping;
- repeated contour;
- damaged scale.

---

## C — THE UNNAMED CENTRE

A material event near the centre or central gravitational zone.

It must resist stable naming.

It should not be built from one source fragment.

Preferred construction:
- at least three materially different fragments;
- at least one hard fragment;
- at least one soft / ambiguous fragment;
- at least one shadow / cavity / negative fragment.

Possible materials:
- rubble;
- cloth;
- concrete;
- skin-like surface;
- dark cavity;
- wet reflection;
- stain;
- metal;
- torn edge;
- dust.

The centre should become less certain the longer it is viewed.

---

## D — THE DARK STRUCTURE

The structural dark field that creates:
- concealment;
- compression;
- hiding;
- emergence;
- spatial depth;
- broken visibility.

It may be formed from:
- apertures;
- walls;
- slabs;
- cloth;
- deep shelves;
- destroyed architecture;
- hard shadow;
- black painted surfaces;
- void-like pockets.

It must not become a neat grid.

---

# 3. ZONES ARE BIASES, NOT BOXES

NIRARA does not have fixed coordinates.

The following are tendencies only:

- **upper / surrounding field:** stronger witness pressure;
- **lower-left / margin:** crying presence;
- **centre / central gravity:** unnamed event;
- **edges / dark pockets:** concealment and partial bodies.

Any composition may violate these positions if the emotional logic improves.

The grammar controls relationships, not coordinates.

---

# 4. ALLOWED ARTIST MOVES

These operations are canonical.

## SCALE
- enlarge beyond realism;
- shrink aggressively;
- make one head too large;
- make another almost disappear.

## CROP
- remove half a face;
- show only an eye;
- cut a body at an uncomfortable point;
- let architecture erase a figure.

## OCCLUDE
- bury fragments behind black forms;
- allow colour or texture to swallow anatomy;
- reveal only what is needed.

## REPEAT
- duplicate a fragment;
- alter scale / rotation / opacity;
- create visual memory without obvious cloning.

## MERGE
- combine several fragments into one impossible form;
- fuse head / wall / cloth / rubble;
- erase seams until category becomes unstable.

## ERASE
- delete information;
- scrape edges;
- remove recognisable features;
- create absence inside a source.

## DISTORT
- stretch;
- compress;
- warp;
- tilt;
- flatten;
- bend perspective;
- damage symmetry.

## RECOLOUR
- contaminate one source with another zone's colour;
- introduce local temperature;
- make black warm, blue, violet, green, brown or red;
- make white dirty, yellowed, cold or flesh-adjacent.

## MATERIAL TRANSFER
- use texture from one object on another;
- let concrete live on a face;
- let cloth texture enter architecture;
- let skin-like tone appear in rubble.

## LIGHT TRANSFER
- steal a highlight from one fragment and use it to bind another;
- create shared illumination across unrelated sources.

## BURIAL
- intentionally lose fragments in darkness, dust, stain or noise.

## REVEAL
- make one small detail unexpectedly sharp inside a soft field.

---

# 5. FORBIDDEN MOVES

These are not banned techniques; they are banned outcomes.

Do not allow the scene to become:

- a tidy collage;
- an asset showcase;
- a moodboard;
- a stock-photo montage;
- a grid of characters;
- a generic horror poster;
- “creepy doll” imagery;
- a clean black UI;
- a symmetrical composition;
- a scene where every object is fully readable;
- a scene where every source keeps its original identity;
- a frame filled only because empty space felt uncomfortable;
- a polished AI-looking dreamscape;
- an image whose complexity comes only from adding more objects.

---

# 6. RICHNESS LAW

NIRARA can be visually dense.

Richness may come from:
- colour contamination;
- layered material;
- tonal variation;
- edge variation;
- reflected light;
- hidden forms;
- repetitions;
- scars;
- stains;
- overlapping depths;
- partially visible figures;
- conflicting temperatures.

Richness does **not** require more object categories.

The artist should first deepen existing relationships before adding another object.

---

# 7. EDGE LAW

Every fragment has an edge.

Edges are part of the composition.

Use a mixture of:
- hard cut;
- soft disappearance;
- torn edge;
- blurred edge;
- dark swallowed edge;
- luminous edge;
- textural erosion;
- partially preserved photographic edge.

If every edge is equally clean, the scene dies.

If every edge is equally blurred, the scene also dies.

---

# 8. DEPTH LAW

The image should contain several perceived depths.

Suggested layers:
- foreground obstruction;
- witness layer;
- central material field;
- deeper cavity / shadow;
- distant residue / light.

Not every composition needs five literal layers.

The requirement is that the scene should not feel pasted onto one flat plane.

---

# 9. COLOUR LAW

Colour is relational.

No fixed palette is mandatory.

But the frame should contain:
- at least one dominant darkness temperature;
- at least one contradictory temperature;
- at least one pale / luminous event;
- local colour contamination between neighbouring fragments.

Examples:
- blue-black + dirty amber;
- warm brown-black + bruised violet;
- green-grey + oxidised red;
- cold cream + deep smoky blue.

These are examples, not presets.

---

# 10. STILL-FIRST LAW

Before blink, sound, interaction or motion:

**the still image must already work.**

Freeze the scene.

If it is dead when frozen, animation will not save it.

A valid still should already contain:
- pressure from the witnesses;
- emotional isolation of the cry;
- attraction / uncertainty at the centre;
- material richness;
- colour tension;
- depth.

---

# 11. VALID-SCENE TEST

A composition belongs to NIRARA when most of the following are true:

- I feel watched before I count the watchers.
- I can locate distress without the scene explaining it.
- The crying presence feels isolated.
- The centre attracts me but I cannot name it confidently.
- The dark areas contain colour and material life.
- The fragments no longer read as independent stock assets.
- The scene has visual richness without random filler.
- Some areas are clear and some are swallowed.
- The composition feels discovered rather than laid out.
- I can stare at the still frame and continue finding relationships.

If the answer is mostly no, the scene is not ready.

---

# 12. ASSEMBLY PROCEDURE

This is a working rhythm, not an instruction manual.

## PASS 01 — THROW
Place fragments quickly.

No polishing.

Find:
- weight;
- pressure;
- emptiness;
- collisions.

## PASS 02 — MOVE
Move pieces aggressively.

Change:
- scale;
- depth;
- overlap;
- direction;
- proximity.

Do not protect the source images.

## PASS 03 — DESTROY
Break source identity.

Crop, erase, distort, stain, merge.

## PASS 04 — BIND
Unify the scene through:
- shared light;
- colour spill;
- material transfer;
- noise;
- shadow;
- edge treatment.

## PASS 05 — REMOVE
Delete anything that only fills space.

## PASS 06 — LISTEN
Stop moving things.

Look at the frame as a painting.

Ask where the eye goes, where it refuses to go, and where the emotional pressure lives.

Then move again only if necessary.

---

# 13. MULTIPLE VALID NIRARA SCENES

The grammar is intentionally capable of producing different compositions.

One scene may emphasize:
- witnessing.

Another may emphasize:
- the cry.

Another may emphasize:
- the unnamed centre.

Another may almost bury all three.

The work remains NIRARA if the underlying relationships survive.

There is no canonical single layout yet.

---

# 14. RELATION TO THE ASSET BANK

The real asset bank supplies **material**.

The Scene Grammar supplies **behaviour**.

No asset has a permanent job.

A fragment is allowed to migrate between roles.

The same piece may appear:
- as witness in one test;
- as centre material in another;
- as texture in another.

This flexibility is intentional.

---

# 15. NEXT PRACTICAL STEP

Build **Assembly Test 01** from a small subset of real fragments.

Do not attempt the final scene.

Use approximately:
- 3–5 witness fragments;
- 2–4 structural fragments;
- 3–5 material fragments;
- 1–2 cry fragments;
- 3+ centre fragments.

The first goal is not beauty.

The first goal is to prove that the pieces can lose their original identity and begin behaving as one living field.
