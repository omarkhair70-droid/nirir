# NIRARA
## MASTER FOUNDATION

**Artist:** Omar Khair  
**Date:** 2026-09-24  
**Status:** CANONICAL FOUNDATION / VISUAL LAW RESET

---

# 1. NAME

**NIRARA**

The name is intentional.

It is not a descriptive title and does not decode the image for the viewer.

Do not replace it with a literal title such as *Unheard*, *Silent Witnesses*, or *The Gap* unless the artist explicitly reopens the naming decision.

---

# 2. CORE IMAGE

NIRARA begins with a visual scene, not with an explanation.

Small reduced beings appear from behind or inside dark forms.

They look.

Some blink.

One small being in the lower-left region cries or calls out with visible intensity.

The cry is loud in its own world, but no one appears to hear it.

At the centre of the composition is an unresolved form / gap / rupture.

It should resist easy naming.

It may be read as a hole, stone, cloth, cavity, mass, absence, or something else.

The work does not decide for the viewer.

---

# 3. FIVE CANONICAL ELEMENTS

Only five elements are required at foundation level.

## 01 — THE DARK FORMS

Dark structural forms.

They may read as:
- boxes;
- partitions;
- apertures;
- screens;
- barriers;
- architectural masses;
- compressed or broken spatial planes.

They are not decorative frames.

They structure hiding, emergence, distance and separation.

They do **not** need to be pure flat black.

Darkness in NIRARA may contain colour, reflected light, texture, residue, depth and material variation.

## 02 — THE WATCHING BEINGS

Small reduced bodies.

They are not anatomically realistic humans.

Canonical traits:
- rounded or near-rounded heads;
- compact or partially dissolved bodies;
- little anatomical detail;
- eyes carry more information than the rest of the body;
- some bodies may be only partially visible;
- their surfaces may carry colour, light, stain, texture or material ambiguity.

They may feel human-adjacent without becoming literal people.

They are witnesses before they are characters.

They must feel **alive**, not diagrammatic.

## 03 — THE BLINK / LOOK

Looking is an event.

Blinking must not become a gimmick or synchronized animation.

Possible states:
- fixed gaze;
- slow blink;
- delayed blink;
- one slightly disturbed / repeated blink;
- almost no movement.

Different beings may carry different degrees of attention.

The gaze should participate in the emotional field, not merely prove interactivity.

## 04 — THE SMALL CRYING BEING

Position bias:
**lower-left / marginal region**.

It should remain smaller and more isolated than the watching group.

This being carries the clearest emotional rupture in the work.

Its cry may be represented through:
- posture;
- mouth / face deformation;
- vibration;
- sound;
- visual pulse;
- colour / light disturbance;
- or a combination.

The scene does not visibly answer it.

Do not surround it with decorative activity.

Its isolation is part of the work.

## 05 — THE UNNAMED CENTRE

A central unresolved presence / absence.

It is not a symbol that must be decoded.

It must remain difficult to classify.

The correct question is not:
“What object is this?”

The correct condition is:
“I cannot quite decide what I am looking at, but the rest of the image seems to organize itself around it.”

It should feel materially and visually rich enough to attract attention without becoming a clean hero object.

---

# 4. INTERNAL TENSION

The work is built from several oppositions:

- watching / being unheard;
- many witnesses / one isolated voice;
- visible attention / absent response;
- dark structure / living material;
- recognisable forms / unnamed centre;
- stillness / blink;
- loud internal distress / external silence;
- darkness / colour;
- density / unresolved space.

These tensions may guide composition.

They are not captions to be shown to the viewer.

---

# 5. WHAT NIRARA IS NOT

NIRARA must not drift into:

- realistic portraiture;
- generic horror imagery;
- cute mascot design;
- random surreal collage;
- sci-fi interface decoration;
- “weird for the sake of weird”;
- crowded symbolism;
- decorative objects inserted because the composition feels empty;
- sterile minimalism;
- flat black emptiness mistaken for seriousness;
- clean graphic design pretending to be atmosphere.

The scene may be strange.

It must not be arbitrary.

The scene may be dark.

It must not be dead.

---

# 6. THE 80 / 20 CUT

The original imagined scene contained a useful warning:

The first ~80% carried a coherent visual and emotional field.

The remaining impulse — throwing unrelated objects into the frame simply to fill it — is rejected.

**Filler is not canon.**

This does **not** mean the image must be visually sparse.

NIRARA may be dense, rich, textured and full of colour relationships.

The distinction is:

**richness is allowed; filler is not.**

Negative space is allowed.

Visual abundance is allowed.

Every part must belong to the same living field.

---

# 7. INTERPRETATION LAW

NIRARA may produce readings around:
- witnessing;
- neglect;
- unheard distress;
- passivity;
- surveillance;
- isolation;
- collective looking;
- emotional distance;
- absence;
- the inability to name what sits at the centre.

None of these is a mandatory explanation.

The artwork should create the conditions for these readings without becoming an illustration of one sentence.

---

# 8. MEDIUM

The medium is not locked yet.

NIRARA may become:
- a browser-based interactive work;
- a moving image;
- a still image / drawing / painting;
- a screen-based installation;
- a physical / digital hybrid.

The final medium must be selected because it strengthens the scene, not because the technology is available.

---

# 9. VISUAL TRUTH CONDITION

Any future execution must preserve:

1. the small watching beings;
2. the marginal crying being;
3. the dark structural forms;
4. the unnamed centre;
5. the discipline against filler;
6. a visibly living field;
7. material richness;
8. colour / light relationships that keep darkness alive.

If an implementation contains all canonical objects but feels dead, flat, sterile or merely “designed,” it has failed.
