# NIRARA — SCENE SCORE v1

**Date:** 2026-09-24  
**Status:** WORKING SCORE  
**Purpose:** A human-readable score for assembling NIRARA by hand.

This is not a template.

It describes pressures and relationships so another person can build a NIRARA scene from the source fragments without copying one fixed layout.

---

## THE FRAME

Think of the frame as a field with four pressures:

### 1. WATCHING PRESSURE
Usually enters from above, behind, or around.

Use several partial presences.

Some are obvious.

Some are nearly lost.

Do not line them up.

Do not make them equally visible.

### 2. CRY PRESSURE
Usually lower and marginal.

Keep it smaller.

Give it emotional force through distortion, colour, texture, posture, sound or light.

Do not let the rest of the scene answer it.

### 3. CENTRAL UNCERTAINTY
Build the middle from multiple incompatible materials.

Do not start with “a hole” or “a rock.”

Start with several fragments and move them until no single name survives.

### 4. DARK PRESSURE
Use darkness to hide, separate and compress.

Darkness may be coloured, reflective, dusty, warm, cold, wet, matte or luminous.

Never use flat black as a substitute for depth.

# FIRST HAND MOVES

When assembling:

1. Throw 10–15 fragments into the frame.
2. Ignore their original categories.
3. Move them until one area starts exerting pressure.
4. Sacrifice at least one “good” fragment by hiding most of it.
5. Make at least one fragment change role completely.
6. Force three different materials to become the unnamed centre.
7. Let colour leak between unrelated fragments.
8. Leave one region unresolved.
9. Remove one thing you originally thought was essential.
10. Stop and look at the still image before adding motion.

---

# WHAT ANOTHER HUMAN NEEDS

A person assembling NIRARA does **not** need to know the intended symbolism.

They need:
- the source fragments;
- the Scene Grammar;
- this Scene Score;
- the anti-deadness rule;
- freedom to move and transform material.

If they can only reproduce one predetermined layout, the system failed.

If they can produce a different but recognisably related NIRARA scene, the system works.

---

# SUCCESS CONDITION

A successful assembly should feel:

- watched;
- materially rich;
- emotionally uneven;
- alive inside darkness;
- difficult to name;
- partially buried;
- unresolved;
- composed by a hand, not filled by a generator.

The result should not look like the sum of its source files.
